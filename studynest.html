StudyNesta little more focus. a lot less juggling.

One welcoming study space for classes, assignments, and focused work. StudyNest brings a weekly timetable, academic to-do list, and Pomodoro timer into a single browser-based companion.

The idea

Three daily needs.
One calmer routine.

Students balance lectures, assignments, and exam preparation—often across different tools. StudyNest explores a simpler setup: bring planning, tracking, and focused study time together so it is easier to see what is next.

The project is built around common student productivity needs. Its discovery themes are design assumptions, not findings from completed interviews or usability tests.

A study day, in five moments

Scroll through the routine.
The desk stays with you.

As each chapter enters view, the StudyNest preview changes mode. This vertical, pinned “study desk” tells the story in a different way from PRAVAHA’s horizontal journey.

01The home base

Start with a clear view.

The dashboard makes the day feel manageable before the first task begins. A quick overview brings important information within reach.

  • Date-aware welcome and a little encouragement.
  • Today's classes and pending work at a glance.
  • A quick path into a focused study session.
02Plan the week

Make time visible, one class at a time.

A weekly timetable gives lectures a home and helps students see how their time is shaped across the week.

  • Seven-day schedule with week navigation.
  • Add, edit, or delete classes with times and locations.
  • Subject colors make the schedule easier to scan.
03Keep tasks moving

Turn deadlines into next steps.

Academic tasks become easier to manage when they have enough context—and progress is visible without digging through lists.

  • Subject, due date, and low / medium / high priority.
  • Mark work complete and filter by timing or status.
  • A progress indicator turns small wins into momentum.
04Find a focus rhythm

Work in a rhythm, not a marathon.

The Pomodoro timer breaks study time into focused work and recovery. Choose a mode, adjust a session, and keep track of time spent.

  • Work, short break, and long break modes.
  • Adjustable durations with pause, reset, and skip controls.
  • Session history and browser notifications when supported and permitted.
05Pick up where you left off

Your routine stays on this browser.

Timetable entries and to-do tasks are saved with browser local storage, so they remain available on the same browser after a refresh.

  • Local persistence for classes and tasks.
  • No account or backend is required for the prototype.
  • Data does not sync across browsers or devices.
Honest boundary: Local storage is browser-specific; this project does not demonstrate cloud sync, authentication, or a backend.

Design & implementation

Built with core web tools. Made for a real routine.

The interface uses a warm cream canvas, navy navigation, and amber accents to feel structured without feeling overly formal. Playfair Display gives headings character; DM Sans keeps everyday interface text clear.

What I practiced

  • DOM manipulation, event handling, and state changes across interface sections.
  • Date and time calculations for timetables and focus sessions.
  • Interactive forms, task filtering, browser notifications, and responsive CSS.
  • Browser local storage for timetable and to-do persistence on one browser.

What I would build next

  • Interview students and usability-test the planner before expanding it.
  • Add accessible mobile navigation, personalized settings, and calendar export.
  • Explore account-based sync, backend storage, and weekly study analytics.
