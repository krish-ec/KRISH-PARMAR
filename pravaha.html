01 — THE OPPORTUNITY

A valuable resource, hard to navigate.

E-waste can contain recoverable materials, but the route from collection to recycling may feel fragmented. PRAVAHA explores how a digital product could make the process easier to understand and coordinate.

  • Price uncertainty makes it harder to judge a fair offer.
  • Finding a suitable recycler can take extra effort.
  • Handover details and material journeys can be difficult to follow.

02 — DISCOVERY

Start with two perspectives.

The concept is shaped around the different needs of collectors and recyclers. Instead of treating them as one audience, it explores separate experiences connected by shared material information.

  • How might collectors identify scrap and understand an indicative value?
  • What information could help recyclers review an incoming batch?
  • How can language, simple interactions and low-connectivity contexts be considered?

03 — THE PRODUCT JOURNEY

From scrap to a clearer next step.

A seven-step journey gives the experience a simple mental model. Each stage makes the next action visible, while keeping the transfer of material at the center.

  • Identify an item and explore its material category.
  • Review an illustrative value range and possible recycler match.
  • Arrange a handover, then represent its record and onward journey.

04 — TWO CONNECTED EXPERIENCES

Different roles. One shared flow.

Collectors and recyclers need different information. The interface concept separates their tasks while keeping material details and handover status connected across the experience.

  • Collector view: identify, understand, arrange and track.
  • Recycler view: review incoming material and batch information.
  • Scanner, price discovery and material-recovery visuals make the concept tangible.
  • Admin concepts support a consistent platform experience.

05 — DESIGNING FOR ACCESS

Make the experience feel approachable.

Visual cues and focused actions help introduce a complex process without overloading the screen. The prototype explores inclusive choices as part of the experience—not as an afterthought.

  • English, Hindi and Marathi interface options.
  • Responsive layouts and light and dark themes.
  • Icon-first patterns and voice-input ideas for exploration.
  • Offline tolerance considered as a requirement, not a verified capability.

06 — IDENTITY WITH INTENT

A name that keeps things in flow.

PRAVAHA draws on the Sanskrit/Hindi word “प्रवाह,” associated with flow or a continuous current. It reflects the idea of making the movement of materials—and the value they carry—easier to follow.

  • Green points to sustainability and resource recovery.
  • Copper connects the visual language to recoverable electronic materials.
  • A leaf motif signals environmental responsibility and circular use.
  • “Your waste has a value” gives the concept a clear, human message.

07 — WHAT I LEARNED

Think beyond a screen. Design the system.

PRAVAHA pushed me to connect product purpose, interface decisions and a shared workflow. It also reinforced the importance of being precise about what a prototype demonstrates—and what still needs validation.

  • Translate a broad problem into a structured user journey.
  • Organize information around distinct user roles.
  • Use interaction and visuals to explain unfamiliar ideas.
  • Separate design intent from production-ready functionality.

08 — NEXT STEPS

A promising concept needs real-world proof.

The next phase would focus on learning from collectors and recyclers, validating the proposed workflow and building the foundations for a dependable service.

  • Interview users and test the flows on real devices.
  • Verify local pricing sources and recycler information.
  • Explore secure accounts, persistent records and reliable QR tracking.
  • Measure successful handovers and material traceability in a pilot.
Prototype note: AI identification, live recycler matching, payments, offline behavior and verified environmental impact are not claimed as production capabilities.
