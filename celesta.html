ACT 01 — THE INVITATION

A first night should feel like an arrival.

The brief: introduce the incoming batch to a freshers' party with the anticipation of a festival campaign, not the feel of a campus announcement.

ACT 02 — THE SIGNAL

Chrome in the dark. Red in motion.

A near-black canvas, sharp display typography and hot-red accents create a nightlife identity. The liquid chrome orb and particle field add movement without hiding the message.

  • Oversized type gives the invitation a clear focal point.
  • Red light cues and metallic gradients carry the event mood.
  • Motion is atmospheric; key details stay readable.

ACT 03 — THE RUN OF SHOW

Show the atmosphere. Make it tangible.

Experience cards preview the night and give students something to imagine: a DJ-led main floor, live acts, a neon arena, a chrome photo booth, crew games and a surprise moment.

  • Short, scannable descriptions keep the lineup easy to explore.
  • Consistent cards help varied activities feel like one event.
  • The surprise stays a teaser, rather than a made-up promise.

ACT 04 — THE DETAILS

Keep the mystery. Lose the guesswork.

Practical information belongs in the same experience as the atmosphere. Event details, entry codes and common questions help students understand what to expect before they arrive.

  • Date and venue are clearly marked as placeholders until confirmed.
  • Entry guidance makes student ID and ticket rules easy to find.
  • FAQ content gives the final decision a straightforward next step.

ACT 05 — THE ENTRY

Build the anticipation. Then make a way in.

The page closes the loop: a countdown builds anticipation, ticket tiers frame the entry options, and a focused call to action points toward joining the night.

  • Countdown timer brings a live sense of occasion.
  • Ticket sections organize the choices and event call to action.
  • Responsive layouts keep the invitation usable on smaller screens.

Behind the experience

One page. A complete night out.

The story moves from anticipation to decision, balancing expressive art direction with event information that can be found quickly.

01 / ARRIVE

Set the scene

A dramatic hero, animated chrome and an event countdown establish the identity from the first screen.

02 / EXPLORE

Picture the night

Event moments, mission details and the dress code turn the concept into a more concrete plan.

03 / ENTER

Make the next step clear

Ticket tiers, entry guidance and FAQs help visitors find the information needed to take action.

THE TAKEAWAY

Event websites work hardest when mood and logistics move together. Celesta uses a strong visual identity to earn attention, then gives the practical details enough space to do their job.
